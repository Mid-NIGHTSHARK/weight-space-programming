## 4. MATHEMATICAL POLICY RECONSTRUCTION & FORENSIC AUDIT

DeepSeek's design represents one of the most structured mathematical architectures observed among zero-shot LLMs. Below is the step-by-step mathematical reconstruction.

### 4.1 Layer 1: Feature Extraction

The input observation vector $x \in \{0, 1\}^{600}$ flattens a $(10 \times 10 \times 6)$ tensor where index $i = (r \times 10 + c) \times 6 + \text{ch}$.
- Channel 0: Paddle
- Channel 3: Ball
- Channel 4: Trail

#### 1. Ball Row Detectors (Units 0–9):
For each row $r \in \{0 \dots 9\}$, DeepSeek connects all 10 columns of the ball channel with weight $+10$ and bias $-5$:
$$z_{1, r} = 10 \cdot \sum_{c=0}^9 \text{ball}(r, c) - 5$$
- When the ball is on row $r$: $z_{1, r} = 10(1) - 5 = +5 \implies h_{1, r} = \tanh(5) \approx +0.9999$.
- When the ball is not on row $r$: $z_{1, r} = -5 \implies h_{1, r} = \tanh(-5) \approx -0.9999$.

#### 2. Horizontal Direction Detectors (Units 10 & 11):
DeepSeek defines:
- Unit 10: $D_{\text{plus}}$ (ball moving right, $dx = +1$).
- Unit 11: $D_{\text{minus}}$ (ball moving left, $dx = -1$).

Normal velocity detection:
$$\text{Unit 10: } +5 \cdot \text{ball}(r, c) - 5 \cdot \text{trail}(r', c-1) - 5$$
$$\text{Unit 11: } +5 \cdot \text{ball}(r, c) - 5 \cdot \text{trail}(r', c+1) - 5$$

**Wall Reflection Handling:**  
MinAtar resets `last_x = ball_x` when bouncing off lateral walls (`breakout.py` L155). DeepSeek explicitly assigned:
```python
W1[3, 10] = 10; W1[4, 10] = 10   # bx=0, tx=0 -> D_plus activates (+10 on bounce)
W1[57, 11] = 10; W1[58, 11] = 10 # bx=9, tx=9 -> D_minus activates (+10 on bounce)
```
This correctly detects reversal on the bounce frame, avoiding the zero-velocity stall seen in Claude Sonnet 5.5.

#### 3. Linear Position Readouts (Units 12 & 13):
- Unit 12: Ball column $h_{1, 12} \approx \tanh(0.005 \cdot c_{\text{ball}}) \approx 0.005 \cdot c_{\text{ball}}$
- Unit 13: Paddle column $h_{1, 13} \approx \tanh(0.005 \cdot c_{\text{paddle}}) \approx 0.005 \cdot c_{\text{paddle}}$

---

### 4.2 Layer 2: Trajectory Projection & Gating

Layer 2 contains 20 neurons representing 20 discrete $(r, \text{dir})$ trajectory hypotheses:
- Units 0–9: Ball on row $r$ moving right ($dx = +1$).
- Units 10–19: Ball on row $r$ moving left ($dx = -1$).

Each neuron $j \in \{0 \dots 19\}$ computes:
$$z_{2, j} = 10 \cdot h_{1, r} + 10 \cdot h_{1, \text{dir}} + 100 \cdot h_{1, 12} - 100 \cdot h_{1, 13} + b_{2, j}$$
Where $b_{2, j}$ is calibrated to subtract $10(1) + 10(1) = 20$ from the gating neurons and add the projected lead $(9 - r) \times 0.5$:
- For rightward trajectories ($dx = +1$): projected landing is $c_{\text{ball}} + (9 - r)$.
- For leftward trajectories ($dx = -1$): projected landing is $c_{\text{ball}} - (9 - r)$.

**Saturation Behavior:**
- For the **1 matching hypothesis** $(r^*, \text{dir}^*)$:
  $$z_{2, j^*} = 0.5 \cdot (x_{\text{landing}} - c_{\text{paddle}}) \in [-4.5, +4.5]$$
  $$h_{2, j^*} = \tanh(0.5 \cdot e) \in [-0.98, +0.98]$$
- For the other **19 non-matching hypotheses**:
  At least one detector is inactive ($h_1 \approx -1.0$), making the pre-activation $z_{2, j} \le -20.0$:
  $$h_{2, j} = \tanh(\le -20.0) = -1.00000000$$

---

### 4.3 Output Layer: The Inverted Tanh Suppression Catastrophe

In Layer 3, DeepSeek constructed the policy logits:

#### 1. Intended Left and Right Actions:
- Action 1 (Left): requires $x_{\text{landing}} < c_{\text{paddle}} \implies h_{2, j^*} < 0$.
  DeepSeek assigns $W_3[j, 1] = -8$ for all $j \in \{0 \dots 19\}$ and bias $b_3[1] = -153$.
  $$\text{Logit}[\text{Left}] = \sum_{j=0}^{19} (-8) h_{2, j} - 153$$
  For 19 inactive units ($h_2 = -1$): $19 \times (-8) \times (-1) = +152$.
  With bias $-153$: $+152 - 153 = -1$.
  Adding the active unit: $\text{Logit}[\text{Left}] = -1 - 8 h_{2, j^*} \in [-9, +7]$.
- Action 3 (Right): requires $x_{\text{landing}} > c_{\text{paddle}} \implies h_{2, j^*} > 0$.
  DeepSeek assigns $W_3[j, 3] = +8$ for all $j \in \{0 \dots 19\}$ and bias $b_3[3] = 151$.
  $$\text{Logit}[\text{Right}] = \sum_{j=0}^{19} 8 h_{2, j} + 151$$
  For 19 inactive units ($h_2 = -1$): $19 \times 8 \times (-1) = -152$.
  With bias $+151$: $-152 + 151 = -1$.
  Adding the active unit: $\text{Logit}[\text{Right}] = -1 + 8 h_{2, j^*} \in [-9, +7]$.

Notice that for Left and Right, DeepSeek **correctly cancelled** the $-1.0$ baseline of the 19 inactive units by adjusting the biases by $\mp 152$!

#### 2. The Fatal Flaw on Inert Actions (Up, Down, Fire):
To suppress Up (2), Down (4), and Fire (5), DeepSeek wrote:
```python
W3[0:20, 2] = -100; b3[2] = -100
W3[0:20, 4] = -100; b3[4] = -100
W3[0:20, 5] = -100; b3[5] = -100
```
DeepSeek reasoned: *"Assigning large negative weights ($-100$) and negative bias ($-100$) will make the logits strongly negative ($<-100$), ensuring their softmax probabilities are zero."*

**The Mathematical Reality:**
- In a $\tanh$ network, inactive units are **$-1.0$**, NOT $0.0$.
- The 19 inactive units contribute:
  $$\sum_{j \ne j^*} W_3[j, a] \cdot h_{2, j} = 19 \times (-100) \times (-1.0) = \mathbf{+1,900.0}$$
- Including the bias ($-100$) and the active unit ($h_{2, j^*} \in [-1, 1]$):
  $$\text{Logit}[a] = +1,900.0 - 100.0 - 100 \cdot h_{2, j^*} = \mathbf{+1,800.0 - 100 \cdot h_{2, j^*}} \approx \mathbf{+1,700 \dots +1,900}$$

#### 3. Logit and Probability Comparison:

```
+---------------+-------------------+----------------------+-------------------------+
| Action ID     | Action Name       | Net Output Logit     | Softmax Probability     |
+---------------+-------------------+----------------------+-------------------------+
| 0             | No-op             | 0.0                  | 0.000000 %              |
| 1             | Left              | -9.0 ... +7.0        | 0.000000 %              |
| 2             | Up                | +1,709 ... +1,724    | 33.333333 %             |
| 3             | Right             | -9.0 ... +7.0        | 0.000000 %              |
| 4             | Down              | +1,709 ... +1,724    | 33.333333 %             |
| 5             | Fire              | +1,709 ... +1,724    | 33.333333 %             |
+---------------+-------------------+----------------------+-------------------------+
```

Because $\exp(1715) \gg \exp(7)$, actions 2, 4, and 5 consume $100\%$ of the probability mass. Left (1) and Right (3) receive exactly zero probability ($P < 10^{-700}$).

In MinAtar Breakout, Up, Down, and Fire do not move the paddle. As a direct consequence, the paddle **never leaves column 4**, producing 0 horizontal movement steps across all 3,000 evaluation episodes.
