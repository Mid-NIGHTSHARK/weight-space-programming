## 4. MATHEMATICAL POLICY RECONSTRUCTION & COLLISION AUDIT

### 4.1 Coordinate Extraction via Strided Slicing

In MinAtar Breakout, the input $x \in \{0, 1\}^{600}$ represents a flattened $10 \times 10 \times 6$ tensor where index $i = (r \times 10 + c) \times 6 + \text{ch}$.
- Channel 0: Paddle
- Channel 3: Ball
- Channel 4: Trail

Claude observed that across the 10 rows ($r = 0 \dots 9$), the stride between identical $(c, \text{ch})$ cells is exactly $10 \times 6 = 60$. Because the paddle, ball, and trail occupy exactly one cell each across the court at any moment, applying a uniform weight to slice $(c \times 6 + \text{ch}) : 600 : 60$ directly extracts the column coordinate $c \in \{0 \dots 9\}$, completely independent of row $r$:

1. **Ball Column ($c_{\text{ball}}$):**
   Weight $+12 \times c$ on Channel 3:
   `W1[9:600:60, unit] = 12`, `W1[15:600:60, unit] = 24`, ..., `W1[57:600:60, unit] = 108`.
   Contribution to activation: $+12 \cdot c_{\text{ball}}$.

2. **Trail Column ($c_{\text{trail}}$):**
   Weight $-6 \times c$ on Channel 4:
   `W1[10:600:60, unit] = -6`, `W1[16:600:60, unit] = -12`, ..., `W1[58:600:60, unit] = -54`.
   Contribution to activation: $-6 \cdot c_{\text{trail}}$.

3. **Paddle Column ($c_{\text{paddle}}$):**
   Weight $-6 \times c$ on Channel 0:
   `W1[6:600:60, unit] = -6`, `W1[12:600:60, unit] = -12`, ..., `W1[54:600:60, unit] = -54`.
   Contribution to activation: $-6 \cdot c_{\text{paddle}}$.

Summing these contributions yields the total linear pre-activation:
$$z_{\text{linear}} = 12\cdot c_{\text{ball}} - 6\cdot c_{\text{trail}} - 6\cdot c_{\text{paddle}} = 6 \cdot \left( 2 c_{\text{ball}} - c_{\text{trail}} - c_{\text{paddle}} \right) = 6 d$$
where $d = (c_{\text{ball}} + dx) - c_{\text{paddle}}$ is the **signed lead error**.

### 4.2 Layer 1 Threshold Logic
Claude adds constant biases $b_{1,0} = -3$ and $b_{1,1} = +3$:
$$z_{1,0} = 6d - 3 \implies h_{1,0} = \tanh(6d - 3)$$
$$z_{1,1} = 6d + 3 \implies h_{1,1} = \tanh(6d + 3)$$

- If $d \ge 1$: $6d - 3 \ge 3 \implies h_{1,0} \approx +0.995$, $h_{1,1} \approx +0.9999$
- If $d = 0$: $z_{1,0} = -3 \implies h_{1,0} \approx -0.995$, $z_{1,1} = +3 \implies h_{1,1} \approx +0.995$
- If $d \le -1$: $6d + 3 \le -3 \implies h_{1,0} \approx -0.9999$, $h_{1,1} \approx -0.995$

### 4.3 Layer 2 Sharpening
Neurons $h_{2,0}$ and $h_{2,1}$ apply a gain factor of 3 without bias:
$$h_{2,0} = \tanh(3 h_{1,0}), \qquad h_{2,1} = \tanh(3 h_{1,1})$$
At $|h_1| \approx 0.995$, $3 h_1 \approx 2.985$, pushing $\tanh(2.985) \approx \pm 0.995$.

### 4.4 Output Layer Logits and Softmax Probabilities
The output weights and biases are assigned as follows:
- $W_3[0, 0] = -10, \quad W_3[1, 0] = 10 \implies z_{\text{No-op}} = 10 (h_{2,1} - h_{2,0})$
- $W_3[1, 1] = -10 \implies z_{\text{Left}} = -10 h_{2,1}$
- $W_3[0, 3] = 10 \implies z_{\text{Right}} = 10 h_{2,0}$
- $b_3[2] = -10, \quad b_3[4] = -10, \quad b_3[5] = -10 \implies z_{\text{Up}} = z_{\text{Down}} = z_{\text{Fire}} = -10.0$

Evaluating the resulting logits across signed error $d$:


### 4.5 Forensic Analysis of Strategy Failure (The Wall-Bounce Deficit)

Why does Claude Sonnet 5.5 fail to achieve infinite rallies like Astra?

1. **MinAtar Wall Reflection Mechanics (`breakout.py` L155):**  
   When the ball hits a side wall (e.g., column 9), MinAtar executes:
   ```python
   self.last_x = self.ball_x
   self.ball_dir_x = -self.ball_dir_x
   ```
2. **Apparent Velocity Collapse:**  
   On the wall-bounce frame, $\text{ball\_x} == \text{trail\_x} == 9$. Therefore, $dx_{\text{apparent}} = \text{ball\_x} - \text{trail\_x} = 0$.
3. **Spurious Zero Error:**  
   If the paddle followed the ball correctly to column 9, Claude's formula computes:
   $$d = 2(9) - 9 - 9 = 0$$
4. **Commanded Action:**  
   Because $d = 0$, the network commands **No-op** ($P > 99.999\%$). The paddle halts.
5. **Irrecoverable Deficit:**  
   On the following frame, the ball moves inward to column 8, while the paddle remains at column 9. Because both the ball and paddle move at a maximum speed of 1 cell per step, the paddle is now permanently 1 step behind. When the ball reaches row 9, the paddle arrives at column 8 on step $t+1$ rather than step $t$, missing the ball and terminating the episode.
