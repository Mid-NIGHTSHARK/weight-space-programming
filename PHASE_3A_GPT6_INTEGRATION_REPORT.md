## 3. WEIGHT PARSER DIAGNOSTICS & IMPLEMENTATION ADAPTATION

### Diagnostic Assessment of GPT-6 Output Format
Unlike Gemini Argon (which generated a markdown code fence ````edits ... ````), GPT-6 formatted its response as:
1. Three introductory prose paragraphs outlining the physical policy concept without markdown backticks.
2. 51 executable Python assignment statements with comments explaining hidden unit roles.
3. Multi-line list literals for 2D matrix assignments (e.g. `W2[0:2, 0:4] = [...]`).

### Parser Bug Identification and Safe Resolution
In `network/weight_parser.py`, the fallback line-by-line parser accumulated tokens into a single string `buffer`. When encountering leading non-assignment prose, `ast.parse` raised a `SyntaxError`, which caused the parser to append subsequent lines into the invalid buffer, poisoning all subsequent lines.

**Sanctioned Parser Fix:**
In `SafeASTWeightParser._parse_line_by_line`:
- When `buffer` is empty, check whether `line.strip()` begins with an allowed tensor target name in `ALLOWED_TENSORS` (`W1`, `W2`, `W3`, `b1`, `b2`, `b3`).
- If not, record the line as `RejectedNonAssignment` in `report.errors` and continue with an empty buffer.
- When an assignment begins, multi-line list continuations are parsed safely until complete.
- Statement validation logic was unified via `self._apply_statement` to guarantee identical AST security, range checking, and operator validation across full-block and line-by-line modes.

## 4. STRUCTURAL NETWORK AUDIT & MATHEMATICAL POLICY RECONSTRUCTION

An exhaustive symbolic and numerical audit was performed on the reconstructed network forward pass:
$$h_1 = \tanh(x W_1 + b_1), \quad h_2 = \tanh(h_1 W_2 + b_2), \quad z = h_2 W_3 + b_3$$

### 4.1 First Hidden Layer ($h_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$)

In Layer 1, exactly two neurons are active: $h_{1,0}$ and $h_{1,1}$. Neurons $h_{1,2 \dots 31}$ have zero incoming weights and zero biases ($\tanh(0) = 0$).

#### Unit 0: Projected-Intercept Error ($q - x_p$)
GPT-6 stated in its comments:
> *For a descending ball at (r, c), the trail identifies horizontal direction: the projected intercept is $c + 9 - r$ when moving right and $c + r - 9$ when moving left. Row- and column-dependent weights encode these expressions without explicitly extracting coordinates.*

**Mathematical Verification:**
The observation flattened index is $i = (r \cdot 10 + c) \cdot 6 + ch$.
- Channel 3 (Ball): $W_1[(r \cdot 10 + c) \cdot 6 + 3, 0] = 0.01 \cdot c \cdot (10 - r)$
- Channel 4 (Trail): $W_1[(r_t \cdot 10 + c_t) \cdot 6 + 4, 0] = 0.01 \cdot c_t \cdot (r_t - 8)$
- Channel 0 (Paddle): $W_1[(9 \cdot 10 + c_p) \cdot 6 + 0, 0] = -0.01 \cdot c_p$

Let $z_{1,0}$ denote the net pre-activation:
$$z_{1,0} = 0.01 \cdot \left[ c_b (10 - r_b) + c_t (r_t - 8) - c_p \right]$$

For normal diagonal descent:
1. **Moving Right & Down:** $r_t = r - 1$ and $c_t = c - 1$.
   $$c(10 - r) + (c - 1)(r - 1 - 8) = 10c - cr + (c - 1)(r - 9) = 10c - cr + cr - 9c - r + 9 = c + 9 - r$$
2. **Moving Left & Down:** $r_t = r - 1$ and $c_t = c + 1$.
   $$c(10 - r) + (c + 1)(r - 1 - 8) = 10c - cr + (c + 1)(r - 9) = 10c - cr + cr - 9c + r - 9 = c + r - 9$$

In both cases, the non-linear cross-product term $-cr + cr \equiv 0$ cancels identically.
The pre-activation simplifies exactly to:
$$z_{1,0} = 0.01 \cdot (x_{\text{proj}} - c_p)$$
Because $x_{\text{proj}} - c_p \in [-18, 18]$, the argument to $\tanh$ is small ($|z_{1,0}| \le 0.18$), where $\tanh(u) \approx u$.

#### Unit 1: Vertical Direction Estimator
- Ball Channel 3 weights: $W_1[\dots, 1] = 1.5 \cdot r_b$ (with boundary adjustments at $r=0$ and $r=8$).
- Trail Channel 4 weights: $W_1[\dots, 1] = -1.5 \cdot r_t$.
- Bias: $b_1[1] = 0.75$.

Pre-activation:
$$z_{1,1} = 1.5 (r_b - r_t) + 0.75$$
- **Descending ball** ($r_b - r_t = +1$): $z_{1,1} = 1.5(1) + 0.75 = 2.25 \implies h_{1,1} = \tanh(2.25) \approx +0.978$
- **Ascending ball** ($r_b - r_t = -1$): $z_{1,1} = 1.5(-1) + 0.75 = -0.75 \implies h_{1,1} = \tanh(-0.75) \approx -0.635$

### 4.2 Second Hidden Layer ($h_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$)

Exactly five neurons are active in Layer 2: $h_{2,0}, h_{2,1}, h_{2,2}, h_{2,3}$, and $h_{2,4}$. Units $h_{2,5 \dots 31}$ have zero weights and zero biases.

#### Units 0..3: Nonlinear Multiplication Gate
$$W_2[0:2, 0:4] = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.5 & -0.5 & -0.5 \\ 0.5 & -0.5 & 0.5 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b_2[0:4] = 1$$

Let $u = 0.5 h_{1,0}$ and $v = 0.5 h_{1,1}$. The four pre-activations are:
$$\begin{aligned}
z_{2,0} &= 1 + u + v \\
z_{2,1} &= 1 + u - v \\
z_{2,2} &= 1 - u + v \\
z_{2,3} &= 1 - u - v
\end{aligned}$$

The combined linear contribution to the output logits (via weights $\pm 140.625$) computes:
$$G(u, v) = \tanh(1 + u + v) - \tanh(1 + u - v) - \tanh(1 - u + v) + \tanh(1 - u - v)$$

By multivariate Taylor expansion around $(1, 0, 0)$:
$$G(u, v) \approx 4 \cdot \frac{\partial^2 \tanh(z)}{\partial z^2} \Big|_{z=1} \cdot u \cdot v = -8 \tanh(1)\operatorname{sech}^2(1) \cdot u \cdot v \approx -1.026 \cdot (h_{1,0} \cdot h_{1,1})$$
This 4-neuron group implements an analytic continuous multiplication gate between horizontal intercept error and vertical direction.

#### Unit 4: Intercept Error Pass-Through
- $W_2[0, 4] = 2.0$, $b_2[4] = 0.0$.
- Pre-activation: $z_{2,4} = 2.0 \cdot h_{1,0}$.
- Output: $h_{2,4} = \tanh(2.0 \cdot h_{1,0})$.

#### Connectivity Audit of Unit 4 and Units 0..3:
- Second-layer unit 4 **is directly connected** to the output logits:
  $$W_3[4, 1] = -30.0 \quad (\text{Action 1: Left}), \quad W_3[4, 3] = +30.0 \quad (\text{Action 3: Right})$$
- Second-layer units 0, 1, 2, 3 **are directly connected** to the output logits:
  $$W_3[0:4, 1] = [+140.625, -140.625, -140.625, +140.625]$$
  $$W_3[0:4, 3] = [-140.625, +140.625, +140.625, -140.625]$$
- **Verification Verdict:** There are NO disconnected features or dead-end paths among active units $h_{2,0 \dots 4}$. Units $h_{2,5 \dots 31}$ are inactive.

### 4.3 Output Layer ($z \in \mathbb{R}^6$)

The net output logits are:
$$\begin{aligned}
z_0 \,(\text{No-op}) &= +2.5 \\
z_1 \,(\text{Left})  &= 140.625 \cdot G(u, v) - 30 \cdot h_{2,4} \\
z_2 \,(\text{Up})    &= -8.0 \\
z_3 \,(\text{Right}) &= -140.625 \cdot G(u, v) + 30 \cdot h_{2,4} \\
z_4 \,(\text{Down})  &= -8.0 \\
z_5 \,(\text{Fire})  &= -8.0
\end{aligned}$$

#### Constructive vs. Destructive Interference:
- **Descending Ball ($h_{1,1} \approx +0.978$):**
  The multiplication gate term $140.625 \cdot G(u, v)$ and the pass-through term $-30 \cdot h_{2,4}$ have the **same sign** and add constructively. For $x_{\text{proj}} - c_p = -5$, $z_1 = +14.27$ and $z_3 = -14.27$, yielding $>99.9\%$ probability of moving Left.
- **Ascending Ball ($h_{1,1} \approx -0.635$):**
  The multiplication gate term flips sign and cancels out the pass-through term (net logit $\approx +0.19$). Because $z_0 = 2.5$, the No-op action dominates ($P(\text{No-op}) \approx 86\%$), keeping the paddle parked while the ball ascends.

### 5.3 Physical Failure Mode Analysis
Why does GPT-6 average 2.49 bricks rather than sustaining long rallies?
1. **Unfolded Linear Geometry:**  
   GPT-6's formula $x_{\text{proj}} = c \pm (9 - r)$ assumes the ball moves in an unbounded plane. When a ball near column 8 moves right from row 4, its calculated intercept is $8 + 9 - 4 = 13$. In MinAtar, the ball reflects off the right boundary at column 9, reversing horizontal velocity and landing at column 5. Because GPT-6 lacks modular boundary folding, the paddle pins itself against column 9 while the ball lands at column 5.
2. **Sticky Actions:**  
   With $p = 0.1$, the environment randomly repeats previous actions. Because GPT-6's margin of error on reflected balls is 0, any inertial slip causes an unrecoverable miss.
