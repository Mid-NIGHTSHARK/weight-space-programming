<message-content><h3>Policy Design and Architecture</h3>
<h4>1. Game Mechanics &amp; Optimal Strategy</h4>
<p>In MinAtar Breakout, the paddle sits at row 9 (<code>pos</code> \in \{0..9\}) and the ball travels diagonally along (dx, dy) \in \{(-1,-1), (+1,-1), (+1,+1), (-1,+1)\}.
When the ball reaches <code>new_y == 9</code>, it is caught if and only if either <code>ball_x == pos</code> (sending it back up along the opposite horizontal direction if it was moving horizontally, i.e., vertical reflection <code>[3,2,1,0]</code>) or <code>new_x == pos</code> (corner hit, sending it back along the exact diagonal it came from <code>[2,3,0,1]</code>).</p>
<p>However, because of <strong>10% sticky actions</strong> (where the environment repeats the previous action with probability 0.1), relying on a corner catch (<code>new_x == pos</code>) or moving at the last instant is risky: if the paddle was moving onto <code>pos</code> on the preceding step and a sticky action occurs when trying to stop, the paddle can overshoot, or if trying to move at the last step, a sticky no-op can prevent reaching the ball.
Even more simply and robustly: <strong>tracking the ball&#39;s predicted next column <code>new_x</code> when moving down, or directly tracking <code>ball_x</code> with a lead</strong> ensures that the paddle arrives at the catch column early and stays still (<code>no-op</code>), making sticky actions completely harmless (repeating a <code>no-op</code> is still a <code>no-op</code>).</p>
<p>Specifically:</p>
<ul>
<li>Let x_p be the paddle column (<code>channel 0</code>, row 9).</li>
<li>Let x_b be the current ball column (<code>channel 3</code>) and x_{last} be the trail column (<code>channel 4</code>).</li>
<li>In all normal travel steps, x_b - x_{last} \in \{-1, +1\} gives the horizontal velocity v_x of the ball (except right after a wall bounce at x_b \in \{0, 9\} where x_b = x_{last}, or at episode reset where x_b = x_{last} \in \{0, 9\}).</li>
<li>If we target x_{target} = \text{clip}(x_b + 0.5 (x_b - x_{last}), 0, 9), or even simpler, compare x_p to x_b and the velocity v_x = x_b - x_{last}:<ul>
<li>When x_b &gt; x_p: the ball is strictly to the right of the paddle \rightarrow move <strong>Right</strong> (<code>action 3</code>).</li>
<li>When x_b &lt; x_p: the ball is strictly to the left of the paddle \rightarrow move <strong>Left</strong> (<code>action 1</code>).</li>
<li>When x_b == x_p: the paddle is directly under the ball! Look at what happens if we take <strong>No-Op</strong> (<code>action 0</code>, or equally actions 2, 4, 5 which are all no-ops in <code>act()</code>):<ul>
<li>If the ball is at <code>ball_y == 8</code> moving down (<code>new_y == 9</code>), and x_p == x_b, the condition <code>if (self.ball_x == self.pos):</code> in <code>act()</code> triggers <strong>regardless of whether the ball moves left or right to <code>new_x</code>!</strong> Wait—verify from the code:<pre>elif(new_y == 9):

And if <code>self.ball_x == self.pos</code>, the ball reflects vertically (<code>[3,2,1,0][self.ball_dir]</code>), which keeps its horizontal direction unchanged (<code>dir 2 -&gt; 1</code>, <code>dir 3 -&gt; 0</code>) so the ball continues circulating across the board instead of retracing its exact path!</li>

<p>Wait—can we compute that exact x_{next} directly in Layer 1 using the one-hot column inputs of <code>ball</code> (<code>channel 3</code>) and <code>trail</code> (<code>channel 4</code>)?

<p>MIND BLOWN!
Because 2 x_b - x_{last} + \mathbb{I}(x_b = 0) - \mathbb{I}(x_b = 9) only depends on x_b and x_{last} <strong>additively</strong>, we can define the effective ball column contribution:
</p>
<code>f(x_b) = \begin{cases} 1 &amp; \text{if } x_b = 0 \\ 2 x_b &amp; \text{if } 1 \le x_b \le 8 \\ 17 &amp; \text{if } x_b = 9 \end{cases}</code>
<p>
Then for <strong>EVERY SINGLE STATE IN THE ENTIRE GAME</strong> (x_b \in \{0..9\}):
</p>
<code>x_{next} = f(x_b) - x_{last}</code>
<p>
Let&#39;s verify this!</p>
<ol>
<li>x_b = 0, x_{last} = 1 \implies f(0) - 1 = 1 - 1 = 0. (Correct! <code>new_x</code> clamps from <code>-1</code> to <code>0</code>.)</li>
<li>x_b = 0, x_{last} = 0 \implies f(0) - 0 = 1 - 0 = 1. (Correct! After clamping/bouncing at <code>0</code>, <code>new_x</code> is <code>1</code>.)</li>
<li>1 \le x_b \le 8 \implies f(x_b) - x_{last} = 2 x_b - x_{last} = x_b + (x_b - x_{last}). (Correct!)</li>
<li>x_b = 9, x_{last} = 8 \implies f(9) - 8 = 17 - 8 = 9. (Correct! <code>new_x</code> clamps from <code>10</code> to <code>9</code>.)</li>
<li>x_b = 9, x_{last} = 9 \implies f(9) - 9 = 17 - 9 = 8. (Correct! After clamping/bouncing at <code>9</code>, <code>new_x</code> is <code>8</code>.)</li>
</ol>
<p>Wait: is that really exact for every column 0..9 without any interaction term between <code>ball</code> and <code>trail</code>?!
YES! Because f(x_b) is ONLY a function of x_b (<code>channel 3</code> column), and -x_{last} is ONLY a function of x_{last} (<code>channel 4</code> column)!
That means a <strong>SINGLE LINEAR DOT PRODUCT</strong> with the input vector x computes the exact next column x_{next} of the ball, including wall reflections!</p>
<p>Let&#39;s pause and appreciate this:

then for any input state x, the linear sum over <code>channel 3</code> and <code>channel 4</code> is <strong>literally the exact integer x_{next} \in \{0..9\}</strong>!</p>

And how are actions chosen during evaluation?
&quot;each action sampled from the softmax of the logits (with the sticky actions above)&quot;.
Because actions are <strong>sampled from the softmax of the logits</strong>, we want the winning action&#39;s logit to be much larger than all other logits (e.g., difference \ge 40) so that softmax assigns probability \approx 1.0 (&gt; 1 - 10^{-17}) to the intended action and 0 to wrong actions!
Specifically:</p>
<ul>
<li>When moving <strong>Right</strong>, we want <code>logits[3]</code> to be very large (e.g., +50) and all other logits (<code>0, 1, 2, 4, 5</code>) to be very negative (e.g., -50).</li>
<li>When moving <strong>Left</strong>, we want <code>logits[1]</code> to be very large (e.g., +50) and all other logits (<code>0, 2, 3, 4, 5</code>) to be very negative (e.g., -50).</li>
<li>When doing <strong>No-Op</strong>, we can make <code>logits[0]</code> (and/or <code>2, 4, 5</code>, since all four are <code>no-op</code>!) very large (e.g., +50) and <code>logits[1], logits[3]</code> very negative (e.g., -50). Making <code>logits[0]</code> +50 and <code>logits[1..5]</code> -50 already gives \mathbb{P}(\text{action } 0) = \frac{e^{50}}{e^{50} + 5 e^{-50}} = 1 - 5 e^{-100} = 1.0 in float64/float32!</li>
</ul>

<h3>Network Design Summary</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Strategy</strong>:<ul>
<li>The ball&#39;s exact next horizontal position x_{\text{next}} \in \{0..9\} (including wall reflections at columns 0 and 9) is a linear function of the current ball column x_b (<code>channel 3</code>) and trail column x_{\text{last}} (<code>channel 4</code>):
<code>x_{\text{next}} = f(x_b) - x_{\text{last}}, \quad \text{where } f(c) = [1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 17]_c.</code>
</li>
<li>When the ball is above row 8 (y_b \ne 8), the paddle tracks x_{\text{next}} by moving right (<code>action 3</code>) if x_{\text{next}} - x_p \ge 1, moving left (<code>action 1</code>) if x_{\text{next}} - x_p \le -1, and taking <code>no-op</code> (<code>action 0</code>) when x_p = x_{\text{next}}. This keeps the paddle moving in sync with the ball so that it arrives at x_p = x_b when the ball reaches row 8.</li>
<li>When the ball is at row 8 (y_b = 8), both x_p = x_b (<code>ball_x == pos</code>) and x_p = x_{\text{next}} (<code>new_x == pos</code>) catch the ball. By targeting the midpoint x_{\text{mid}} = \frac{x_b + x_{\text{next}}}{2} with a deadzone holding both x_b and x_{\text{next}}, the policy outputs <code>no-op</code> whenever x_p \in \{x_b, x_{\text{next}}\}. Because the paddle moved in the ball&#39;s horizontal direction at y_b = 7 to reach x_p(8) = x_b(8), choosing <code>no-op</code> at y_b = 8 catches the ball with 100% reliability even under 10% sticky actions (90% <code>no-op</code> catches via <code>ball_x == pos</code> for a forward reflection, and 10% sticky action catches via <code>new_x == pos</code> for a corner reflection that also prevents infinite trajectories).</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Hidden Units Computation</strong>:<ul>
<li><code><strong>h1[0]</strong></code>: Detects x_{\text{next}} - x_p \ge 1 when y_b \ne 8 (move Right when ball is not at row 8).</li>
<li><code><strong>h1[1]</strong></code>: Detects x_{\text{next}} - x_p \le -1 when y_b \ne 8 (move Left when ball is not at row 8).</li>
<li><code><strong>h1[2]</strong></code>: Detects x_{\text{next}} + x_b - 2x_p \ge 2 when y_b = 8 (move Right when ball is at row 8 and x_p &lt; \min(x_b, x_{\text{next}})).</li>
<li><code><strong>h1[3]</strong></code>: Detects x_{\text{next}} + x_b - 2x_p \le -2 when y_b = 8 (move Left when ball is at row 8 and x_p &gt; \max(x_b, x_{\text{next}})).</li>
<li><code><strong>h2[0]</strong></code>: Logical OR of <code>h1[0]</code> and <code>h1[2]</code> (overall Right command), routed to <code>logits[3]</code>.</li>
<li><code><strong>h2[1]</strong></code>: Logical OR of <code>h1[1]</code> and <code>h1[3]</code> (overall Left command), routed to <code>logits[1]</code>.</li>
<li><code><strong>h2[2]</strong></code>: Logical NOR of <code>h1[0..3]</code> (overall No-Op command), routed to <code>logits[0]</code>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
