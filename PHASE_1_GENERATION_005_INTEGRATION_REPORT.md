## 4. MATHEMATICAL RECONSTRUCTION & POLICY MECHANICS

GPT-6 Luna designed a mathematically transparent feedforward policy to track the ball's projected horizontal position.

### Layer 1: Linear Column Encoding

GPT-6 Luna uses three hidden units in Layer 1 to extract the horizontal coordinate of the ball, the trail, and the paddle:
- **Unit 0 (Ball, Channel 3):**
  $$z_{1,0} = \sum_{r=0}^{9} \sum_{c=0}^{9} W_1[r \times 60 + c \times 6 + 3, 0] \cdot \mathbf{x}[r, c, 3]$$
  For column $c \in \{0 \dots 9\}$, weights are set uniformly across all rows to:
  $$W_1[\cdot, 0] = 0.08 \times (c - 4.5) \in \{-0.36, -0.28, -0.20, -0.12, -0.04, +0.04, +0.12, +0.20, +0.28, +0.36\}$$
  Because the ball occupies exactly one grid cell, $z_{1,0} = 0.08 \times (c_{\text{ball}} - 4.5)$. With small magnitude ($|z| \le 0.36$), $\tanh(z) \approx z$.

- **Unit 2 (Trail, Channel 4):**
  Identical weights mapped to channel 4:
  $$z_{1,2} = 0.08 \times (c_{\text{trail}} - 4.5)$$

- **Unit 4 (Paddle, Channel 0):**
  Identical weights mapped to channel 0 (row 9 paddle cells):
  $$z_{1,4} = 0.08 \times (c_{\text{paddle}} - 4.5)$$

### Layer 2: Lead-Target Tracking Error

Layer 2 contains a single active neuron:
- **Unit 0 ($W_2$):**
  $$W_2[0, 0] = +12.0, \quad W_2[2, 0] = -6.0, \quad W_2[4, 0] = -6.0, \quad b_2[0] = 0.0$$
  The pre-activation evaluates to:
  $$z_{2,0} = 12 \cdot h_{1,0} - 6 \cdot h_{1,2} - 6 \cdot h_{1,4}$$
  Substituting the small-angle linear approximation $h_1 \approx z_1$:
  $$z_{2,0} \approx 12 \times 0.08 (c_{\text{ball}} - 4.5) - 6 \times 0.08 (c_{\text{trail}} - 4.5) - 6 \times 0.08 (c_{\text{paddle}} - 4.5)$$
  $$z_{2,0} \approx 0.96 (c_{\text{ball}} - 4.5) - 0.48 (c_{\text{trail}} - 4.5) - 0.48 (c_{\text{paddle}} - 4.5)$$
  $$z_{2,0} \approx 0.48 \times (2 c_{\text{ball}} - c_{\text{trail}} - c_{\text{paddle}})$$
  Notice that the centering offsets cancel out exactly:
  $$0.48 \times [2(-4.5) - (-4.5) - (-4.5)] = 0.48 \times [-9.0 + 4.5 + 4.5] = 0.0$$
  Therefore:
  $$z_{2,0} \approx 0.48 \times d, \quad \text{where } d = 2 c_{\text{ball}} - c_{\text{trail}} - c_{\text{paddle}}$$

### Layer 3: Logits and Softmax Probabilities

Layer 3 maps $h_{2,0} = \tanh(z_{2,0})$ to output actions:
- **Weights ($W_3$):** $W_3[0, 1] = -4.0$ (Left), $W_3[0, 3] = +4.0$ (Right), all others $0.0$.
- **Biases ($b_3$):** $b_3[0] = +1.5$ (No-Op), $b_3[2] = -6.0$ (Up), $b_3[4] = -6.0$ (Down), $b_3[5] = -6.0$ (Fire).

The resulting logits are:
$$\text{Logit}[\text{No-Op}] = +1.5$$
$$\text{Logit}[\text{Left}] = -4.0 \cdot h_{2,0}$$
$$\text{Logit}[\text{Right}] = +4.0 \cdot h_{2,0}$$
$$\text{Logit}[\text{Up}] = \text{Logit}[\text{Down}] = \text{Logit}[\text{Fire}] = -6.0$$

## 6. MECHANISTIC FAILURE MODE ANALYSIS

Why did GPT-6 Luna Extra High achieve a mean score of 1.253—outperforming DeepSeek (0.484) and random chance (0.485), but falling short of GPT-6 Original (2.493) and Sonnet 5.5 (10.957)?

Detailed inspection of episode failure traces revealed two primary mechanistic limitations:

### 1. One-Step Lead Tracking vs Bottom-Row Intercept Projection
- **Theoretical Target:** GPT-6 Luna set its lead target to $\hat{x} = 2 \times \text{ball\_x} - \text{trail\_x} = \text{ball\_x} + dx$. This predicts where the ball will be **on the very next step ($y+1$)**, NOT where the ball will land when it reaches the paddle at row 9 ($y=9$).
- **Consequence:** When the ball is high up on the board (e.g., row 2 or 3), the paddle moves left and right mirroring the ball's instantaneous column rather than moving ahead to the bottom-row bounce intercept. By the time the ball reaches row 8, the paddle often needs 3 to 4 steps of travel to intercept the ball, but only has 1 or 2 steps remaining before the ball passes row 9.

### 2. Wall Reflection Saturation & Turnaround Lag
- When the ball strikes the left wall ($c_{\text{ball}} = 0, c_{\text{trail}} = 1$) or right wall ($c_{\text{ball}} = 9, c_{\text{trail}} = 8$), the next step projection evaluates to $2(0) - 1 = -1$ or $2(9) - 8 = 10$.
- Because $-1$ and $10$ lie outside the valid grid range $[0, 9]$, the paddle drives hard into the side wall. Once the ball reflects and moves toward the center, the trail does not immediately reflect the new direction until the subsequent frame, producing a 1–2 frame delay during which the paddle continues steering into the wall.
- Combined with sticky actions ($p=0.10$), this lag frequently causes the paddle to miss balls that bounce off side walls within 3 rows of the paddle.
